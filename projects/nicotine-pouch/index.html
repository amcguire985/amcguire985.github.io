Child-resistant nicotine pouch container

Apr – May 2024 · CDP

Redesign of a nicotine pouch container requiring child-resistant closure while remaining usable for dexterity-impaired adults. Delivered three detailed production-ready designs in 6 weeks, leading to client patent applications.

[Placeholder: Concept 1 CAD render]

Concept 1: Push-turn locking mechanism. Combines audible/tactile feedback with barrier to inadvertent opening. Patent filed on locking feature.

[Placeholder: Concept 2 CAD render]

Concept 2: Dual-action sequential clasp. Requires simultaneous pressing of two points. Ergonomic study showed acceptable force levels for target user.

[Placeholder: FDM prototype assembly]

FDM-printed prototypes of all three concepts. Tested with 8 user participants across age groups 65–80 with varying dexterity levels.

[Placeholder: Final injection moulding drawing]

Production-ready tooling drawing for leading concept. Optimized for 2-cavity mould with minimal undercuts. Includes parting line, gate, and runner design.

My role

Lead designer for this user-centred product development project. Planned and facilitated two ideation sessions with cross-functional team (client, packaging engineers, user researchers). Downselected concepts using multi-criteria decision matrix. Owned detailed CAD and engineering drawings for injection moulding manufacture across all three final designs. Coordinated user testing with dexterity-impaired adults to validate openability and establish force/torque requirements.

Key technical achievements

Balanced child resistance (16 N opening force per standard ASTM D3079) with usability for 65+ age group (validated through user testing). Minimal material complexity — all three designs achievable in single elastomer resin with overmoulded rigid cap. Eliminated metal components to reduce recycling complexity and cost. All three designs fast-tracked to patent applications within 4 weeks of project completion.
